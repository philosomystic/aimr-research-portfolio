Human–AI Developmental Research

HAIDR Overview

Toward a design science of human–AI developmental ecologies.

5-Minute HAIDR Overview →Full Scholarly Paper →Research Sites & Studies →

The research problem

Human–AI research already investigates learning, cognitive offloading, function allocation, agency, organizational change, collective intelligence, and longitudinal effects. The unresolved problem is increasingly one of integration and inference: which configurations produce which durable consequences, under what conditions, and across which levels and timescales?

HAIDR is a candidate integrative research architecture, not a comprehensive theory. Its value depends on whether connecting established literatures improves explanation, measurement, comparison, and design beyond the component approaches alone.

Nested developmental ecologies

A human–AI developmental ecology is the organized set of conditions through which recurrent human–AI participation is structured and developmental consequences become more or less likely. Within it, local configuration and specified participation protocol condition what occurs; enacted participation topology records the actual relational organization of consequential activity. These operate within task, social, organizational, institutional, material, and prior-history conditions.

From protocol to enacted participation

Nominal task allocation can conceal different distributions of consequential operations. “Human review” might involve independent judgment before AI exposure, targeted anomaly detection, substantive correction, or procedural approval. These arrangements provide different opportunities to practice judgment, revise, and learn.

The program distinguishes specified participation protocol from enacted participation topology: who actually initiates, retrieves, generates, interprets, judges, monitors, corrects, decides, and acts. Longitudinal records of consequential operations can then test how recurrent participation relates to later capacity and strategy-space change.

Three interacting dimensions

DimensionQuestion
Ecological levelWhat nested conditions select, constrain, or stabilize participation?
Developmental processHow do configurations distribute operations, organize recurrent participation, and shape multilevel outcomes?
TimescaleWhat changes within episodes, across repeated episodes, and over longer trajectories?

Microgenetic change occurs within episodes. Mesogenetic patterns stabilize, differentiate, transfer, or reorganize across repeated episodes. Longitudinal / ontogenetic trajectories involve more durable shifts in repertoire, expertise, agency, dependence, role, and realizable participation.

The relation is recursive: changed capacities alter later participation, roles, workflows, and institutional arrangements.

The recurring evaluative problem: sustained capability

What capability changed, where does it now reside, what does it depend upon, and what happens when conditions change?

Present task performance, supported human–AI capability, retained human competence, practical intervention capability, higher-order orchestration, distributed expertise, organizational capability, resilience, and infrastructure dependence are distinguishable outcomes.

Unaided performance is not a universal gold standard. Stable complementary support can be valuable; delegation can enable higher-order human capacities. Claims about human development nevertheless require evidence beyond successful supported performance, including delayed retention, transfer, perturbation, and scaffold reduction or withdrawal.

Configuration discovery → causal discrimination → configuration optimization

Prospective methodological direction · September 26, 2026

The space of possible human–AI participation configurations is enormous. How can researchers discover which configurations are worth experimentally testing? As sufficiently rich longitudinal datasets become available, large-scale interaction traces combined with sufficiently capable AI systems may permit computational developmental ecology mining: reconstructing participation topology from raw archives and relating recurrent configurations to subsequent capability trajectories.

Potential dimensions include goal formulation; human/AI initiative; planning, execution, monitoring, correction, challenge, and synthesis; explanation and scaffolding; friction; memory and persistence; user expertise; and task characteristics. These could be related to later strategy choice, independent performance, transfer, metacognitive calibration, conceptual differentiation, problem-solving repertoire, and agency/autonomy—with passive-sensing or neurobehavioral measures where appropriate.

Which recurrent distributions of cognitive operations predict unusually large subsequent increases in independently demonstrated capability?
  1. Configuration discovery. Naturalistic interaction traces → participation-topology reconstruction → candidate developmental configurations. AI-assisted coding, representation learning, statistical analysis, and appropriate causal-discovery methods could identify recurrent structures associated with later developmental outcomes. This stage generates hypotheses; causal-discovery algorithms do not substitute for experimental identification.
  2. Causal discrimination. Candidate configurations → discriminating causal hypotheses → prospective longitudinal experiments testing durable developmental effects. Manipulate relevant participation structures while holding appropriate task/system characteristics approximately constant. Test delayed independent performance, transfer, perturbation, scaffold withdrawal, strategy-space expansion, metacognitive change, and agency/autonomy. Supported performance ≠ development. Observational scale increases discovery power; prospective experimentation provides causal discrimination.
  3. Configuration optimization. Once causal mechanisms become sufficiently credible, factorial designs, sequential experimentation, adaptive trials, contextual bandits, Bayesian optimization, or related approaches may support systematic search for developmentally productive configurations. The mature question is: given person P, task T, developmental objective D, and context C, what participation configuration maximizes durable developmental gain?

One potential HAIDR contribution is configuration ontology + developmental outcome architecture + hypothesis-generation system: determining which distinctions and contrasts are scientifically worth manipulating, without needing to conduct every large-scale experiment itself. This work requires serious competence in causal inference, longitudinal design, measurement, experimentation, human–AI interaction, and developmental theory. It is one route toward cumulative empirical design knowledge about durable, transferable change.

This methodological refinement is prospective: it does not imply that HAIDR currently possesses large-scale longitudinal datasets, has validated the pipeline, or has demonstrated transformative configurations.

Bounded research sites and conditional design knowledge

AIMR, organizational systems, neuroscience and passive sensing, collective intelligence, and Wunderhorn investigate different portions of the architecture. Study 0 is AIMR’s current empirical anchor and is hypothesis-generating. Prospective measurement work and interventions are described separately in Research Sites & Studies.

The longer-term ambition is progressively stronger conditional design knowledge: which configurations produce which capability trajectories, for whom, under what conditions, across which levels and timescales, and with which tradeoffs?

Epistemic boundaries

Individual performance cannot establish collective intelligence. Neural adaptation cannot establish beneficial development. Musical structure cannot validate a human-developmental mechanism. Coherence, corrigibility, carry-forward, component contribution, and perturbational adequacy remain provisional organizing distinctions whose value requires comparison with established alternatives.

Collective adaptive capacity is the societal horizon. It does not replace the bounded empirical work needed to establish relations among ecology, configuration, participation, capability, and trajectory.
