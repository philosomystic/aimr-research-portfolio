Candidate Developmental & Augmentation Variable Architecture (CDAVA)

A provisional architecture for specifying what might change through sustained human–AI participation, where capacities reside, and how developmental trajectories can be distinguished.

AIMR is developing not only a theory of developmental change, but a candidate architecture for specifying and testing what kinds of capacities are amplified, internalized, distributed, displaced, or reorganized through sustained human–AI participation.

The architecture addresses a central empirical problem:

When a human–AI system becomes more capable, what—if anything—has changed in the human?

Improved coupled performance does not by itself establish human development. CDAVA provides an architecture for distinguishing immediate augmentation from changes that become persistent, transferable, independently available, distributed across the coupling, displaced, or reorganized at a higher level.

1. Experimental ontology

CDAVA distinguishes several analytically different levels of the developmental problem. Features of the developmental ecology can be configured; those configurations shape how human–AI participation unfolds; participation and augmentation processes may alter human capacities; and apparent changes must then be evaluated for where they reside, whether they persist and transfer, and what developmental trajectory they produce.

The resulting experimental ontology is:

Configuration variables
Model · continuity · memory · protocol · tools · interface/ecology · task
↓ influence
Participation / augmentation processes
↓ potentially alter
Candidate developmental capacities
↓ evaluated through
Capacity location · persistence · transfer · perturbation · withdrawal
↓ yielding
developmental trajectory

This distinction prevents experimentally configurable conditions, immediate augmentation, human developmental change, and longitudinal outcomes from being collapsed into a single category of “AI effects.”

Architecture at a glance

CDAVA — From AI participation to developmental trajectory. Configurable features of the developmental ecology influence participation and augmentation processes; those processes may alter candidate human capacities; developmental claims then depend on capacity location, persistence, transfer, perturbation robustness, and scaffold withdrawal before a trajectory can be classified.

The remainder of this page unpacks each level of this architecture and the relations among them.

2. Configuration variables

Core developmental-ecology configuration space:

Model · Continuity · Memory · Interaction protocol · Tools · Interface / ecology · Task

These are upstream features of the ecology, not developmental outcomes. Human capacities and intentions, prior developmental history, and broader social-material conditions can additionally function as participant/context moderators.

3. Participation / augmentation processes

Within a configured ecology, recursive participation may include:

reflection → articulation → questioning → planning → feedback → revision → action → recurrence

Candidate augmentation processes

cognitive-load redistribution · exploratory-time compression · representational augmentation · generative augmentation · evaluative augmentation · activation-chain compression · reflective continuity

Coupling properties

delegation topology · human participation density · initiative allocation · scaffold density/adaptivity · evaluative authority · monitoring and correction responsibility

These processes can improve coupled performance without necessarily producing human development.

4. Candidate developmental capacities

Cognitive / epistemic

Metacognition · differentiation · integration · developmental coherence · epistemic corrigibility · learning / transfer

Self / agency

Self-modeling · self-efficacy · judgment · initiative · executive organization · developmental self-regulation

Relational / embodied participation

Relational competence · embodied/ecological regulation · meaningful participation

Measurement stance

Where mature constructs and validated measures already exist, AIMR should borrow them rather than rename them.

Supported performance ≠ development

Better performance while AI support is present is an intermediate observation, not evidence by itself of durable human development.

5. Capacity-distribution topology

H — Human-internalized capacity

Increasingly available independently, including after scaffold reduction.

C — Coupling-dependent augmentation

Reliably available through human–AI participation but substantially reduced without the coupling. This can represent beneficial complementary offloading rather than developmental failure.

E — Emergent co-intelligent capacity

Best explained as a capability of the recurrent human–AI system rather than either component alone.

Mixed / indeterminate

Partially distributed or dynamically changing across H/C/E over time.

How should capacity be distributed across person, AI, and environment so that the overall ecology increases human developmental freedom without creating unacceptable displacement, dependency, or loss of contact?

6. Developmental tests

Supported → Independent → Delayed → Transfer → Perturbation → Scaffold withdrawal

Developmental claims strengthen when change persists across time, transfers across tasks or contexts, remains robust under altered conditions, survives appropriate scaffold reduction or withdrawal, is independently retrieved or reconstructed, and remains corrigible by contact with realities outside the AI interaction.

7. Developmental trajectories

Capacity amplification

AI-supported participation contributes to stronger human capacities that increasingly persist independently.

Stable complementary offloading

Some functions remain coupled, but delegation is intentional, beneficial, evaluable, and developmentally acceptable.

Capacity displacement

Coupled performance rises while important independent human capacity declines or becomes inaccessible without support.

Upward reorganization of agency

Lower-level operations are delegated while substantive higher-order capacities develop: goal selection, strategy, orchestration, evaluation, uncertainty management, correction, ethical judgment, and intervention.

Emergent co-intelligent capacity

A stable capability develops at the level of recurrent participation without straightforward reduction to independent human capacity or simple offloading.

8. Delegation topology

The developmental consequences of AI assistance depend not only on how much is delegated, but on what is delegated.

goal selection → problem framing → information gathering → option generation → planning → execution → monitoring → evaluation → correction → completion → reflection

Automate what is developmentally expendable while preserving active participation in operations that build the capacities one cares about.

9. Developmental freedom and adaptive regulation

AIMR does not assume that the best developmental outcome is maximum internalization or minimum technological reliance. A more general criterion is developmental freedom: whether the resulting ecology expands meaningful capacity to configure, evaluate, revise, reduce, withdraw from, or deliberately sustain forms of mediation and coupling.

What configuration of mediation, scaffolding, delegation, friction, continuity, and world-contact best supports durable human agency while taking full advantage of beneficial co-intelligence?

Adaptive regulation can therefore involve increasing or fading scaffolding, preserving productive friction, delegating expendable operations, reintroducing human participation, protecting evaluative authority, strengthening world contact, or deliberately retaining beneficial coupling.

10. Ecological consequences

wellbeing · embodiment · relationships · meaningful agency · world contact · sustainability

A cognitive or productivity gain may still be developmentally mixed if it creates unacceptable dependency, chronic overextension, relational withdrawal, or displacement of embodied/environmental experience.

11. Evidence architecture

E1 — Phenomenological
Experienced changes in cognition, affect, perception, agency, meaning, or self-understanding.

E2 — Conversational-behavioral
Observable changes in discourse, reasoning, differentiation, criticism, revision, retrieval, and interaction.

E3 — Enactive
Changes expressed through consequential participation in work, relationships, health, projects, environments, and ordinary life.

E4 — Withdrawal / transfer
Persistence, generalization, spontaneous reuse, perturbation robustness, and performance under reduced scaffolding.

C1 — External corroboration
Independent observations capable of corroborating or contradicting E1–E4.

12. CDAVA within the empirical program

CDAVA is a provisional, high-recall variable architecture—not a fixed confirmatory codebook and not a single omnibus scale.

Its current empirical role is:

Study 0R → Study 0R-X → CDAVA-informed construct differentiation → Study 0P → comparative / experimental program

Retrospective reconstruction surfaces candidate changes. External digital traces triangulate selected claims. CDAVA differentiates constructs and capacity-location hypotheses. Prospective probes test persistence, transfer, perturbation, and withdrawal. Later experiments manipulate configuration variables across people, systems, tasks, and contexts.

The central empirical object is:

the longitudinal reorganization of capacities, agency, and participation across a recursively coupled human–AI developmental ecology.
