Neuroscience & Passive Sensing

Potential mechanistic evidence for distinguishing coupling-dependent performance, durable human change, reorganized strategy, and behavioral transfer.

The candidate neuroscientific object

Neural adaptation to recurrent distributions of cognitive operations within human–AI developmental ecologies.

The corresponding question is whether repeated participation in different human–AI ecologies alters the neural organization by which a person subsequently performs cognitive operations. Direct evidence remains preliminary.

Convergence across evidence streams

  • Participation history: which operations were performed by whom, in what sequence, and under what support?
  • Behavioral change: strategy use, error detection, spontaneous initiation, correction, and independent enactment.
  • Transfer and perturbation: novel tasks, changed models, conflicting information, reduced support, or AI withdrawal.
  • Passive traces: timing, revision, task switching, information seeking, conceptual reuse, communication rhythms, and—where appropriate—device or physiological measures.
  • Neural evidence: repeated fMRI, EEG, or other appropriate measures during independently performed tasks.

Triangulation, not a standalone criterion

Neural change does not establish beneficial development. Passive sensing does not independently establish wellbeing or capability. Context dependence, missing data, external validity, interpretability, standardization, and incremental predictive value must be addressed.

The strongest inference would combine participation history, longitudinal traces, behavioral change, transfer, perturbation or withdrawal, and neural evidence. Ethical and methodological appropriateness govern whether a sensing channel belongs in a study.

Research status

This is a bounded research site for prospective mechanistic triangulation, not a claim that HAIDR has already collected or validated neural or passive-sensing measures.
