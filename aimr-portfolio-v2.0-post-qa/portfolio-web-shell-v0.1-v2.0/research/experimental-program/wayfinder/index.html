Prospective Research

Wayfinder

Adaptive developmental environments · AI-mediated learning

Can adaptive challenge, productive friction, and progressively calibrated scaffolding create conditions in which human capacities develop through repeated enactment, transfer, and increasing agency?

Candidate experimental and design program

Wayfinder would investigate AI-mediated environments that dynamically organize developmental challenges around the evolving capacities of the participant. Rather than gamifying performance through points, badges, or external rewards, the system would treat development itself as the progression architecture: increasingly differentiated challenges, productive friction, adaptive scaffolding, repeated enactment, scaffold reduction, and opportunities for transfer into new contexts.

Development as the progression architecture

The design object is the environment through which a participant repeatedly exercises and reorganizes capacities. Progress would concern how the participant takes up a challenge, uses support, integrates feedback, initiates subsequent action, and brings a capacity into a new context.

Challenge and support would be calibrated together. A task can become easier because the system performs more of it; that change must be distinguished from the participant becoming more capable. Productive friction preserves the cognitive work through which a capacity may develop while scaffolding supports participation.

The adaptive learning environment

  1. Adaptive challenge: organize tasks around the participant’s evolving capacities and the demands of the activity.
  2. Productive friction: preserve opportunities to differentiate, judge, revise, and act.
  3. Adaptive scaffolding: calibrate the form and degree of assistance to the operation being practiced.
  4. Repeated enactment: revisit capacities across episodes and changing challenges.
  5. Scaffold reduction: examine what the participant can increasingly initiate and sustain with less support.
  6. Transfer: create opportunities to use emerging capacities in unfamiliar contexts and consequential activity.
  7. Increasing agency: evaluate the participant’s growing role in directing and evaluating their own participation.

This sequence expresses the proposed design logic. It is not a fixed developmental stage model: a new challenge may require renewed support, and transfer may reveal distinctions that reorganize subsequent practice.

What would be tested?

The empirical program would examine how configurations of challenge, friction, and scaffolding change the cognitive operations enacted by the participant. AI can perform an operation, co-reason, teach, prompt, critique, withhold assistance, or progressively fade a scaffold. These roles create different opportunities for practice and initiative.

Immediate assisted success would be compared with later independent transfer, spontaneous initiation, adaptability, and scaffold dependence. The relevant question is whether capacities become more stable and available across conditions, including after AI assistance is reduced or removed.

Candidate developmental targets

Differentiation, integration, metacognition, coherence, and agency provide candidate targets for challenge design and measurement. Their operationalization would depend on the task and context. Transfer is an explicit part of the research design, rather than an assumption inferred from progress within the environment.

Empirical logic

Repeated observations would connect supported participation and enactment with subsequent tests of persistence, transfer, perturbation, and scaffold reduction. Interaction traces can describe participation; repeated probes, behavioral measures, and self-report can contribute complementary evidence about change.

  • Which configurations of adaptive challenge and scaffolding support capacity formation across repeated episodes?
  • When does productive friction support differentiation, integration, and agency, and when does the challenge require recalibration?
  • Which operations become spontaneously initiated as scaffolds are reduced?
  • Do capacities practiced within the environment transfer into new tasks, contexts, and everyday participation?
