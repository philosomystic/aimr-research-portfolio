Prospective Research

Wunderhorn Project

Parallel experimental laboratories · Developmental intervention · Longitudinal experiment

Can the generative constraints of coherent development be discovered experimentally—and can that knowledge inform the deliberate cultivation of human capacities under sustained AI participation?

Candidate prospective research program · Musical laboratory active · Human-development laboratory prospective

The Wunderhorn Project connects two experimental laboratories: an active investigation of musical ontogeny and a prospective study of human development under sustained AI participation. Their shared problem concerns the conditions under which a developing system can change coherently while preserving identity, continuity, and the capacity for further development.

Laboratory I: Experimental musical ontogeny

The musical laboratory begins with Mahler’s Wer hat dies Liedlein erdacht? as a coherent musical organism. Reverse-engineering identifies the organization through which a small set of musical materials sustains a larger developmental whole: identity carriers and cells, ecological roles, regenerative fields, transformation rules, behavioral primitives, grammar and syntax, developmental states, and constraints.

The aim is to move from describing an existing work to experimentally investigating its generative organization. Which features carry identity? Which relationships can move? What must remain invariant for a transformed descendant to retain perceptible ancestry and functional continuity?

Controlled intervention

  1. Freeze a control. Preserve the source organization against which a descendant can be compared.
  2. Specify the transformation in advance. Identify the variable to change and the features to hold invariant.
  3. Make a minimal, resolution-matched intervention. Begin at a scale at which the proposed mechanism can be isolated.
  4. Compare ancestor and descendant. Examine perceptible ancestry, functional continuity, developmental mobility, and reproductive capacity.
  5. Progressively expand the intervention. Move from event to role, network, process, and organism as the simpler transformations become intelligible.

The longer-range question is whether a new musical organism can develop from minimal identity cells under generative constraints: an experimental approach to artificial ontogeny, rather than variation alone.

Laboratory II: Human development under AI participation

The prospective human-development laboratory translates the experimental logic into the deliberate cultivation of human capacities. Candidate mechanisms include differentiation, recognition, integration, metacognition, conceptual reorganization, and agency.

A baseline establishes what the participant can already do. Targeted AI scaffolds then support particular operations across repeated episodes. Subsequent assessment asks whether those operations become more stable, spontaneously initiated, transferable, and resilient to reduced support.

  1. Establish baseline human capacities.
  2. Introduce a targeted AI scaffold or developmental intervention.
  3. Track repeated enactment and microgenetic change.
  4. Assess delayed availability and spontaneous initiation.
  5. Introduce novel transfer tasks and controlled perturbations.
  6. Reduce or withdraw the scaffold and evaluate what remains available.

The analysis distinguishes AI-supported performance, stabilized coupled capacity, and candidate human-internalized capacity. A successful supported episode does not establish that the human has acquired the capacity.

A bridge of experimental method

The correspondence between the laboratories is methodological. It makes intervention, continuity, transformation, and persistence inspectable across different systems without assuming that musical and human development share an identical mechanism.

The critical asymmetry

A human participant brings intentions, experience, judgment, and agency to the study. Preserving musical identity is not equivalent to preserving human agency. Translation between the laboratories therefore generates hypotheses and experimental questions; evidence for human developmental change must come from the human-development study itself.

Research questions

  • Musical development: Which generative constraints preserve identity while permitting coherent transformation? Which roles are mobile, and which are constitutive?
  • Human development: Which configurations of AI participation support differentiation, recognition, integration, metacognition, and agency? Which support internalization, and which sustain scaffold dependence?
  • Developmental persistence: What becomes spontaneously available, transfers to new contexts, and survives delay, perturbation, and scaffold reduction?
  • Methodological translation: How should the scale of an intervention match the scale of the proposed mechanism, and which principles survive translation between laboratories?

Current status

Candidate prospective research program. Musical laboratory active; reverse-engineering phase completed in first form and controlled-intervention methodology under development. Human-development laboratory remains prospective, informed by Study 0, developmental-measurement work, and methodological findings emerging from the musical laboratory.
