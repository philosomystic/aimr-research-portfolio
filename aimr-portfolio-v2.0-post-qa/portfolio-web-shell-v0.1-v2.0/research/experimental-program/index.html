HAIDR · Experimental Program

Experimental Program

Developmental-ecology configuration variables, controlled manipulations, and developmental trajectory tests.

Within the Human–AI Developmental Research Program (HAIDR), the Experimental Program treats features of the human–AI developmental ecology as configuration variables that can be systematically varied to test their effects on participation, augmentation, candidate human capacities, and developmental trajectories.

Which configurations produce which longitudinal distributions of human and coupled capacity?

Prospective Research

From developmental theory to experimental intervention

The Prospective Research Program translates HAIDR’s theoretical architecture into empirical studies, developmental interventions, measurement systems, and designed human–AI environments.

What configurations of human–AI participation cultivate durable human capacities—and how can we know when development has actually occurred?

Read the program overview

The program begins from a central distinction: enhanced performance within a human–AI system is not equivalent to development of the human participant. AI may enable a person to accomplish something they could not accomplish alone without producing a corresponding change in human capacity. Conversely, sustained participation with AI may create conditions in which initially scaffolded cognitive organizations become increasingly available to the person across time, contexts, and reductions in support.

The prospective program investigates this distinction experimentally. Across different domains and research designs, studies examine how developmental ecology, participation topology, scaffolding, repeated enactment, recognition, integration, productive friction, and the distribution of cognitive operations affect developmental trajectories.

The central empirical logic is:

developmental ecology → recursive participation → differentiation → recognition → integration → coherence → agency → longitudinal development

The strongest evidence of human development is therefore not improved AI-assisted performance alone, but increasingly stable capacities that are spontaneously initiated, transferable to novel situations, resilient under perturbation, and available when AI support is reduced or removed.

Four candidate projects currently explore complementary parts of this research space:

Wunderhorn investigates developmental constraints through parallel experimental laboratories. Developmental Dashboard develops the measurement and feedback architecture needed to make longitudinal change legible. Wayfinder investigates adaptive developmental environments designed around challenge, productive friction, and progressive scaffolding. AI-Mediated Research Methodology Acquisition examines whether sophisticated AI-supported activity can become independent human expertise.

Together, the projects move HAIDR from the question “What happens under sustained AI participation?” toward the experimental question:

What configurations of human–AI participation cultivate durable human capacities—and how can we know when development has actually occurred?

Developmental Ecology: Experimental Configurations. Model, continuity, memory, interaction protocol, tools, interface/ecology, and task are developmental-ecology configuration variables. Experiments vary these inputs, observe participation and supported performance, then reduce or alter scaffolding to test persistence, transfer, capacity location, and developmental trajectory.

hold task/system relatively constant → manipulate developmental ecology → observe supported performance → alter or withdraw scaffolding → test persistence, transfer, and human-capacity change

Capacity location, perturbation response, and the resulting developmental trajectory then inform the next configuration.

The seven sections below define families of manipulable configuration variables, not seven isolated causal claims. The mature program can examine interactions among them—for example, continuity × memory, protocol × model behavior, interface × task—once simpler effects are understood.

1. Model

Capabilities, style, reliability, epistemic behavior, and other characteristics of the artificial participant.

Research question

How do differences in model behavior shape the developmental processes elicited through sustained human–AI participation?

More specifically: do models that differ in responsiveness, challenge, uncertainty calibration, explanatory capacity, or epistemic resistance produce different trajectories of differentiation, integration, judgment, and agency?

Manipulation

Vary selected model properties while holding the task, interaction protocol, and other ecological conditions as constant as possible.

Candidate contrasts include differences in model capability, response style, degree of challenge, expressed uncertainty, epistemic resistance, or tendency toward affirmation.

Developmental outcome

Changes in human:

differentiation · critical judgment · articulation · metacognition · corrigibility · independent reasoning

The relevant outcome is not simply whether one model produces better answers, but whether repeated participation with it changes what the human becomes able to do.

Developmental test

Compare supported performance during interaction with subsequent unassisted, delayed, and transfer performance.

Test whether capacities practiced with one model remain available when the model is removed, replaced, or behaves differently.

Key tension

responsive coherence ↔ epistemic resistance

A highly responsive model may accelerate recognition and integration while also increasing the risk of false coherence. Developmentally productive interaction may require enough resistance to preserve discrimination and corrigibility.

2. Continuity

The stability and persistence of human–AI interaction across time.

Research question

Does sustained interactional continuity enable cumulative developmental change that episodic interaction does not?

Continuity may allow distinctions, unresolved questions, prior corrections, and emerging capacities to remain active across episodes. But does this accumulation produce greater human development—or primarily a more capable coupled system?

Manipulation

Compare conditions with differing levels of continuity, for example:

episodic interaction ↔ conversational continuity ↔ synthesized longitudinal continuity

Hold model, task domain, and overall interaction exposure as comparable as possible.

Developmental outcome

Changes in:

integration across episodes · spontaneous reuse · conceptual stability · metacognition · agency · cumulative learning

Particular attention goes to whether previously developed distinctions become increasingly available without explicit reintroduction.

Developmental test

After sustained interaction, remove or disrupt continuity and test:

delayed recall · independent articulation · novel transfer · spontaneous reuse · performance after continuity loss

If benefits collapse when accumulated conversational history disappears, the developmental locus may remain largely within the coupled system rather than the human.

Key tension

continuity ↔ dependence

Continuity may support cumulative development precisely because the system increasingly carries the history of development. The empirical problem is determining when that external continuity supports internal change—and when it substitutes for it.

3. Memory

What the system retains, retrieves, synthesizes, and reintroduces across interactions.

Research question

How does AI memory affect what becomes integrated into the human participant rather than merely preserved externally?

Does persistent system memory strengthen developmental continuity by making previous learning available for elaboration, or reduce the need for the human to consolidate and retrieve it independently?

Manipulation

Vary memory architecture while holding other conditions constant.

Possible conditions include:

no persistent memory · archival retrieval · conversational memory · synthesized developmental memory

The manipulation can also vary how actively prior material is surfaced versus requiring the human to reconstruct it.

Developmental outcome

Changes in:

retention · integration · self-generated retrieval · conceptual organization · spontaneous reuse · independent synthesis

A central measure is whether the participant increasingly carries forward relevant structure without the system having to supply it.

Developmental test

Reduce or remove access to external memory after repeated use and assess:

independent reconstruction · delayed transfer · novel application · spontaneous recall · performance under memory ablation

Compare what the coupled system remembers with what has become available to the human participant.

Key tension

memory ↔ human internalization

External memory can increase the intelligence of the activity system while simultaneously reducing pressure for human retention. HAIDR therefore distinguishes improved longitudinal coordination from human developmental change.

4. Interaction Protocol

How initiative, turn-taking, challenge, feedback, roles, scaffolding, and responsibility are distributed across the interaction.

Research question

Which structures of human–AI participation increase the human participant's capacities rather than simply increasing the quality of jointly produced outputs?

This is the most direct experimental expression of the participation-topology proposition that amount of AI assistance is a poor descriptor of agency; what matters is the topology of participation.

Manipulation

Systematically vary who performs key cognitive functions such as:

problem formation · exploration · generation · discrimination · evaluation · synthesis · correction · decision

For example, compare answer-giving, guided questioning, adversarial dialogue, fading scaffolds, or human-first versus AI-first reasoning.

Developmental outcome

Changes in:

independent inquiry · discrimination · synthesis · metacognition · judgment · self-authored agency

The crucial variable is not merely performance quality but whether the human increasingly performs functions previously supplied by the scaffold.

Developmental test

Progressively reduce AI participation and test whether the human can independently:

formulate problems · generate distinctions · evaluate alternatives · detect errors · synthesize conclusions · correct course

Use novel tasks and delayed probes to distinguish learned capacity from adaptation to a particular protocol.

Key tension

assistance ↔ productive friction

and, at the deeper level,

agency support ↔ agency displacement

A scaffold can lower unnecessary cognitive cost while preserving the work through which capacities develop—or remove precisely the work that would have generated development.

5. Tools

Search, retrieval, representations, analysis systems, external extensions, and action affordances incorporated into the human–AI ecology.

Research question

How does access to external tools change the distribution and development of human capacities within a coupled human–AI system?

Do tools expand the range of problems through which humans can develop competence and agency, or increasingly relocate essential cognitive operations outside the person?

Manipulation

Compare otherwise similar interaction conditions with different tool affordances, such as:

conversation only · external search/retrieval · persistent representations · analytic tools · action/execution tools

Specific studies should isolate tool functions rather than treating “tool access” as a single undifferentiated variable.

Developmental outcome

Depending on the tool, outcomes may include:

research competence · synthesis · verification · planning · problem decomposition · monitoring · practical agency

HAIDR is particularly interested in whether tools permit participation in previously inaccessible tasks strongly enough for new capacities to develop.

Developmental test

Remove the tool after repeated supported participation and assess what remains:

independent strategy use · task transfer · conceptual understanding · error detection · execution competence

Development may also appear as improved ability to select, direct, and evaluate tools, rather than reproduce everything they previously performed.

Key tension

extension ↔ offloading

The relevant question is not whether cognition is externally distributed—it routinely is—but whether repeated distribution expands or erodes the human capacities required for effective independent and future participation.

6. Interface / Embodied Context

Device, modality, posture, mobility, setting, interruption structure, latency, privacy, environmental access, and proximity to consequential action.

Research question

How does the embodied and material organization of AI interaction alter developmental outcomes?

Does the same conversational system produce different trajectories when interaction occurs at a desk, through mobile or voice interfaces, during action, in social settings, or in direct contact with the environments about which reflection is occurring?

Manipulation

Vary selected dimensions of the embodied-interface ecology while keeping the underlying task and model comparable.

Candidate contrasts include:

text ↔ voice · stationary ↔ mobile · screen-mediated ↔ environmentally integrated · reflective separation ↔ action-proximal interaction

Developmental outcome

Changes in:

integration with action · embodied awareness · contextual transfer · independent engagement · environmental contact · practical agency

This card extends HAIDR beyond linguistic performance by asking whether reflective change reorganizes participation in the lived world.

Developmental test

Assess whether capacities demonstrated during AI interaction transfer into:

unmediated situations · ordinary behavior · interpersonal contexts · physical environments · action without immediate AI access

Measure not only what participants can articulate about change, but whether participation outside the interface changes accordingly.

Key tension

embodiment ↔ conversational enclosure

AI can bring reflective support closer to lived action. It can also pull attention and regulation into an increasingly self-contained mediated environment. A developmental ecology should therefore be evaluated partly by whether mediation preserves, deepens, redirects, or displaces world contact.

7. Task

The goals, complexity, structure, novelty, duration, and evaluative demands of the activity.

Research question

How do developmental effects vary across tasks, and which apparent changes represent transferable capacities rather than task-specific adaptation?

The developmental inference criterion is that development cannot be inferred from improvement on the activity in which assistance was originally provided.

Manipulation

Systematically vary task characteristics while holding the relevant human–AI configuration constant.

Candidate dimensions include:

familiar ↔ novel · structured ↔ ambiguous · low ↔ high complexity · reflective ↔ practical · near-transfer ↔ far-transfer

Tasks can also be designed to require different combinations of articulation, differentiation, synthesis, judgment, or action.

Developmental outcome

Changes in capacities such as:

problem formulation · discrimination · synthesis · metacognition · judgment · adaptive transfer · agency

The aim is to identify whether a developmental effect is capacity-specific, domain-specific, or more broadly generalizable.

Developmental test

Move progressively away from the training ecology:

same task → structurally related novel task → new domain → delayed real-world application

Test whether performance survives scaffold reduction while the surface characteristics of the task change.

A strong developmental signal is not merely doing the practiced task better, but bringing an acquired capacity to bear where the original scaffold and problem structure are no longer present.

From configuration to developmental trajectory

Different developmental ecologies may change what cognitive work the human performs, what becomes repeatedly practiced, where initiative is located, which operations are delegated, which forms of productive friction are preserved or removed, and how much evaluative authority remains with the human.

The target is therefore not necessarily maximum independent human performance. Positive trajectories can include both human internalization and stable complementary coupling, provided the latter remains intentional, evaluable, and compatible with developmental freedom.

Candidate trajectory classes

  • Capacity amplification
  • Stable complementary offloading
  • Capacity displacement
  • Upward reorganization of agency
  • Emergent co-intelligent capacity
How should capacity be distributed across person, AI, and environment so that the overall ecology increases human developmental freedom without creating unacceptable displacement, dependency, or loss of contact?
