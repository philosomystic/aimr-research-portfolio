Collective Intelligence

What is this group or institution becoming capable of, and what forms of independent expertise, correction, and adaptive capacity remain distributed within it?

Individual augmentation does not guarantee collective improvement. A human–AI arrangement can improve local performance while changing the distribution of strategies, judgments, errors, and expertise across a group.

The evolving architecture of collective capability

Relevant questions concern epistemic diversity, correlated judgments and errors, distributed expertise, information aggregation, centralization of epistemic dependence, deliberation, and coordination.

Shared AI support may help people access knowledge or generate hypotheses. It may also make apparently independent judgments more correlated. Short-term organizational efficiency may coexist with reduced opportunities to cultivate the expertise needed for later correction.

Evidence across levels

These possibilities require direct cross-level study. Individual experiments cannot establish collective effects. Evaluation must examine the group or institution’s own performance, knowledge distribution, correction, and response to changing conditions over time.

Collective adaptive capacity is the wider societal horizon. It is not an outcome already established by a reflective-dialogue case or a set of individual productivity gains.

Research status

This page defines the bounded site and its inferential questions; it does not report a completed collective-intelligence study.
