AIMR · Bounded research site

AI-Mediated Reflection

What forms of AI participation help a person become a better reflector rather than merely giving the person better reflections?

AIMR is a bounded research site within HAIDR. Attention, interpretation, differentiation, metacognition, conceptualization, self-questioning, and meaning-making can be observed in dialogue and tested for persistence and transfer.

The distinction is between useful AI-generated reflection, better reflection while using AI, and durable human reflective capability arising through repeated participation. AIMR builds alongside established reflective-agency and self-regulated-learning research.

AIMR across developmental timescales

Microgenetic: within an episode

An episode may move from experience or an unresolved problem to an initial human formulation, an AI probe or counterexample, differentiation, recognition, revision or integration, and provisional action. Analysis asks who generated a distinction, what prompted reorganization, and whether the person discriminated among alternatives rather than simply adopting an interpretation.

Recognition-Cycle Subloop

An externally supplied distinction does not become human capacity merely by appearing in dialogue. The candidate recognition cycle involves:

  1. Attention: this may matter.
  2. Discrimination: what exactly is being distinguished?
  3. Comparison with experience and evidence.
  4. Acceptance, rejection, or refinement.
  5. Reformulation in the person’s own conceptual organization.
  6. Application to the immediate case.
  7. Later spontaneous recognition under altered conditions.

This is a HAIDR/AIMR hypothesis. It can fail through fluent repetition without discrimination, premature labeling, deference to AI authority, or failure to transfer. The empirical issue is whether AI-supplied content becomes independently discriminable and reusable.

Mesogenetic: across repeated cycles

Across days, weeks, or months, do distinctions recur spontaneously? Does initial human formulation become more precise? Does the person generate counterexamples and recognize inadequate AI interpretations? Does scaffolding fade, or does interpretive dependence increase? The distribution of reflective work is tracked across repeated episodes.

Longitudinal / ontogenetic: reflective agency

Longer trajectories concern independently initiated reflection, conceptual repertoire, self-correction, transfer, authorship of meaning, higher-order coordination of AI assistance, dependence, and real-world decision and action. Repeated use alone does not establish development.

AIMR Participation Protocol

The protocol is the structured distribution of reflective operations, initiative, epistemic authority, and scaffolding across time. The complete architecture is a proposal, not a validated instrument.

1. Problem ownership
Who identifies the problem worthy of reflection?
2. Initial formulation
Does the person articulate a view before receiving AI interpretation?
3. Generative responsibility
Who generates hypotheses, meanings, possibilities, interpretations, or explanatory frames?
4. Question topology
Does AI clarify, discriminate, seek evidence, offer counterfactuals, shift perspectives, or lead the person toward an interpretation?
5. Timing of conceptual introduction
Does AI name a construct before or after the person differentiates the phenomenon?
6. Ambiguity management
Is uncertainty preserved long enough to discriminate among possibilities?
7. Challenge structure
Does AI affirm, critique, offer counterexamples, expose missing evidence, or model rival interpretations?
8. Epistemic authority
Is AI treated as an interpreter, hypothesis generator, critic, collaborator, or source of candidate perspectives?
9. Human revision requirement
Must the person actively reformulate after challenge?
10. Recognition requirement
Must the person identify evidence, distinguish alternatives, and restate the pattern in their own terms?
11. Alternative generation
Are competing interpretations generated before or after AI assistance?
12. Evidence and grounding
Is reflection tested against behavior, events, documents, other people, or observable outcomes?
13. Action coupling
Does reflection generate real-world tests capable of producing new evidence?
14. Continuity and memory
What is remembered across episodes, and how does memory affect interpretive authority?
15. Scaffold adaptation
Does assistance adapt over time toward greater human generation?
16. Withdrawal
Are there intervals without AI assistance?
17. Transfer testing
Are strategies tested on novel problems, outside reflective dialogue, or after delay?
18. Meta-reflection on the coupling
Does the person examine how AI is shaping interpretation, agency, dependence, and vocabulary?

Illustrative experimental contrasts

  • AI-led: AI identifies themes, interprets, reframes, synthesizes, and recommends.
  • Dialogic: human formulation precedes AI probing, differentiation, challenge, and requests for evidence.
  • Developmental: scaffolding adapts over time, increasing demands for human generation, recognition, revision, independent enactment, and transfer.

These are contrasts to investigate, not an established ranking of effective protocols.

Current empirical anchor: Study 0

Study 0 is an intensive longitudinal case combining retrospective reconstruction and external digital-trace triangulation with prospective reasoning probes, delayed transfer, perturbation, and scaffold reduction or withdrawal. It is hypothesis-generating rather than evidence sufficient to validate HAIDR.
