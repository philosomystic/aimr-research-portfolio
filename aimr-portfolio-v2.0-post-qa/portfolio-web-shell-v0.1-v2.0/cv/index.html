RESEARCH

AI-Mediated Reflection (AIMR)

Founder / Independent Researcher · 2025–present

Developing an interdisciplinary research program examining developmental change through sustained recursive human–AI participation within a broader design science of developmental ecologies.

Current theoretical and empirical work focuses on:

  • developmental trajectories across microgenetic, mesogenetic, and longitudinal timescales;
  • differentiation, integration, coherence, agency, and internalization;
  • participation topology—the distribution of cognition, initiative, judgment, responsibility, and learning across human–AI systems;
  • developmental continuity, memory, scaffolding, embodiment, and ecological configuration;
  • distinguishing supported performance from persistent and transferable human development;
  • persistence, transfer, scaffold withdrawal, perturbation, and independent reuse as developmental tests.

Research Program

Study 0: Intensive Longitudinal Case Study of AI-Mediated Development

Principal Investigator / Independent Researcher · 2026–present

Theory-generating intensive longitudinal study using a sustained human–AI interaction archive and prospective developmental probes to identify candidate developmental trajectories and generate empirically testable hypotheses.

Current components include:

  • Study 0R — Retrospective Reconstruction: longitudinal reconstruction of candidate developmental trajectories across archived interaction episodes;
  • Study 0P — Prospective Developmental Probes: repeated research-reasoning probes using independent baselines, delay, transfer, perturbation, scaffold reduction, and withdrawal;
  • development of a structured episode-coding matrix;
  • microgenetic analysis of discrimination, synthesis, articulation, metacognition, adversarial criticism, transfer, research-problem formulation, and agency.

Study 0

AIMR Experimental Program

Research Program Development · 2026–present

Developing a controlled experimental program treating features of human–AI interaction as candidate developmental variables.

Current experimental configuration space:

Model × Continuity × Memory × Interaction Protocol × Tools × Interface/Embodied Context × Task

Core methodological logic:

hold task/system constant → manipulate ecology → measure supported performance → reduce scaffold → test persistence/transfer

Current study families examine model behavior, continuity, memory architecture, participation structure, tool use, embodied/interface context, and task transfer.

Developing an interactive research landscape positioning AIMR within the emerging literature on long-term human–AI interaction and development.
